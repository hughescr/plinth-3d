Craig 2026-10-21, looking at Deinonychus throat-feather support (arm 196, t1 c18, refused straight drop at 0.299 mm vs 0.3 mm final gap): "as soon as that support leaves the immediate landing area on the feathers, it's completely clear of the model and could drop at pretty much any angle to the plate or to any nearby other post... it's never gonna approach closer to the model than it already is... That's what the angled head is for: to let the support arm get 'away' from the model at the best departure angle to clear the model, and then once clear, be much more free to head in just about any direction that doesn't approach closer to the model than it already is... for model-to-model supports probably some similar approach would work where the landing spot on the model is treated in the same way as the 'base'. ... I'd guess [the 'now just head down' point is] maybe a few mm from the contact point."

**Why:** the 0.3 mm gap was being enforced against the model right beside the support's own contact, where the tip already touches; that refused sensible straight drops and forced full-length leans.
**How to apply:** near each model contact (tip or model landing), clearance is judged as departure: the arm must move away and never get closer to the model than at departure; past the clear point the normal 0.3/0.6 rules apply and straight-down/nearby landings win. ChituBox has no measure tool, so Craig judges by eye — give distances in pictures. Related: [[two-tier-clearance-ruling]], [[plate-packing-fine-model-end-access]].
