# CALIBRATION COUPON PLATES — bench reading guide

Generated by `generate_coupons.py` from `params.py`. Machine: **ELEGOO Saturn 4 Ultra 16K**. Resin: **Sunlu Water-Wash ABS-Like Black**, 0.050 mm layers, 1.2 s normal / 3x15.0 s bottom + 5 transition.

## 0. Before you slice — read this once

1. Open the `.chitubox` file. The field is **already positioned**. **Do NOT press Arrange / Auto-layout / Center.** Auto-arrange introduces sub-0.02 mm translation jitter and would destroy the Y-position experiment, which is the entire point of plate 1.
2. **Turn support generation OFF.** Every specimen stands on its own 2.00 mm pad. There is not one generated support anywhere in the design and adding any would invalidate the plate.
3. Slice with the Sunlu WW ABS Black profile **unchanged** — same exposure, same anti-aliasing (on), same layer height. The plate fits an empirical failure envelope, so calibration conditions must equal production conditions exactly.
4. Check the on-screen model position reads X 0.00 / Y 0.00 and that the model sits flush on the plate with no lift and no supports.
5. Confirm which screen direction is +Y before you print. The plate self-identifies anyway (see §0.1), but knowing beforehand is free.

### 0.1 How the printed plate tells you which way round it is

**The answer is different on plate 1 and on plates 2-4, because the row marker tiles are gone from 2-4.** Craig read plate 1 and reported the markers "completely superfluous — I didn't even use them just tossed them in the trash after curing", so they were removed rather than reprinted: 8.63 ml of resin carrying nothing that is not already inside the specimen IDs.

**On EVERY plate, including 2-4 — the universal cue:**

* **Every row carries a family-E cure-witness slab whose ID's trailing digit is that row's front-to-back index.** Plate 1 `E1X1`…`E1X5`, plate 2 `E2X1`/`E2X2`/`E2Y1`/`E2X3`/`E2Y2`/`E2X4`/`E2X5` front to back, plate 3 `E3X1`…`E3X4`, plate 4 `E4X1`…`E4X6`. `verify_plates.py` enforces one witness per row on every plate, so this cue cannot go missing without the build failing. **One E slab in the frame fixes Y.**
* Every specimen ID is **globally unique across the whole set**, so a stray part is always placeable: plate 2 = `B**` / `F[ELF]nn` / `WN` / `E2*`, plate 3 = `C**` / `CL*` / `CH*` / `CS*` / `WT` / `E3*`, plate 4 = `G*` / `X**` / `E4*`. (Plate identity used to be embossed on one header per plate; the ID prefix carries it now.)
* Redundant seconds, free: on **plate 2** the last character of every B-cell ID is its station (`…1` = front row, `…5` = rear row, `…3` = one of the three main rows, whose e-letter `A`/`B`/`C` = 1.30/2.60/5.20 mm says which) and the two family-F rows split by rung parity (odd rungs front, even rear). On **plate 4** the ID reads `G<station>…`, and `X01`…`X10` exist only in the rear row. On **plate 3** the four rows are unequally spaced (-42.3 / -21.1 / +2.3 / +28.6 mm) with a rear-only ballast band at +48.4, so a 180 deg rotation is unmissable.
* A true **mirror** (as opposed to a rotation) is caught by the embossed text on every specimen — mirrored glyphs are the least ambiguous cue there is, and unlike a fiducial they are in every frame. On plate 4 the towers add a second check: fingers cantilever **+X**, the witness ledge protrudes **-X**.

**Plate 1 only (it is the frozen printed record and keeps what it was printed with):**

* Every row starts (at -X) with a **header tile** carrying the row name in 5 mm characters plus a row of **tick bars counted from the FRONT: 1 bar = front-most row, N bars = rear-most**. The front-most header also reads `FRONT`, the rear-most `BACK`, and the middle one `PLATE1`.
* Four **3 x 3 mm corner fiducials** (triangle = front, square = back). They are GONE from plates 2-4: at 3 x 3 mm they fall straight through the 1 cm holes in the wash basket. Nothing on plates 2-4 is smaller than 10.5 mm in either axis.

## 0.2 Read protocol (this is not optional — it is where the data is)

**The measurement is the PRE-WASH photograph.** Everything after it exists to prove that handling did not corrupt the pre-wash read. If you do only one thing carefully, do step 3.

1. **Drain with the plate still in the machine, parts hanging down**, until runoff stops. Resin runs down over the specimens and drips back into the vat instead of being wasted on a tray, and drainage is more complete than it would be parts-up. Do not remove anything yet.
2. **Then remove the plate and flip it parts-up.** Do not photograph in situ: on a Saturn the parts hang under the plate and the 15 mm masts occlude each other completely from the side.

   > **FLIPPING INVERTS Y — the axis the whole experiment measures.** Recover it from the **family-E cure witness that is in every row frame by construction** (`verify_plates.py` enforces one per row on every plate): its ID's trailing digit is the row's index counting from the **FRONT (-Y**, where release starts) to the **BACK (+Y**, nearest the hinge) — plate 1 `E1X1`…`E1X5`, plate 2 `E2X1`/`E2X2`/`E2Y1`/`E2X3`/`E2Y2`/`E2X4`/`E2X5`, plate 3 `E3X1`…`E3X4`, plate 4 `E4X1`…`E4X6`. On plate 2 the station digit at the end of every B-cell ID and on plate 4 the `G<station>` prefix say the same thing redundantly; on plate 1 the header tick bars (fewest = front) and the corner fiducials (triangle = front, square = back) do too. **The row markers were removed from plates 2-4 and are NOT the cue any more.** A row that cannot be assigned to its Y station contributes nothing to the position fit.

   > **Wait a minute after flipping before shooting.** Residual resin re-flows when inverted and a film bridging a 0.166 mm gap reads as a present finger. A gentle puff of air across the row clears it.

3. Photograph straight down, **one frame per row** (not one frame of the whole plate — the smallest graded feature is 0.166 mm and a full-plate frame will not resolve it). Lighting is the whole game here; see §0.2.1.
4. **This is the data.** Either score the tables below from these photographs, or just send the photographs — see §0.2.2 for both routes.
5. **Then remove the parts and wash them loose.** You do NOT have to wash the plate intact: every specimen is labelled, so identity survives removal. Photograph again after wash + dry, **before** post-curing, and diff the two sets. The difference is exactly the handling/washing damage that every previous design simply assumed to be zero. Any specimen that changes state between the two photographs is scored `WASH` and excluded from the fit — and the count of those is itself the answer to "print-time or post-print?".
6. **Post-cure last, and photograph a third time only if you want it.** Cure AFTER the diff photo, not before: post-cure raises strength 1.3-3.2x (Riccio) and can visibly curl a 0.166 mm finger, either of which would contaminate a pre/post-wash comparison that is supposed to isolate handling damage.
7. For the **3-5 boundary specimens of each family only**, take a 5-10x macro shot of the break surface. A peel-bend break is a flat transverse face at the notch; a suction/cupping failure is a dished, drawn surface over a broad area; a delamination is a stepped, layer-parallel separation. Eye-reading cannot tell these apart and the failure MODE is a separate deliverable from the failure BOUNDARY.
8. **Filter the resin and inspect the film before the next plate.** These plates are designed to shed specimens into the vat; that is the measurement working. A cured fragment sitting on the film during subsequent layers is how a PFA install gets damaged.

### 0.2.1 Lighting — angle and direction both matter

**Background:** ignore any advice about a white background for the on-plate shots. The plate IS the background and it is grey. Contrast comes from the light, not the backdrop. A white tray does help for the post-wash shots, where the parts are loose and you can choose.

**Angle: 40-50 degrees above the plate surface.** Not grazing. Shadow length = mast height / tan(angle), and the masts are 15 mm:

| light angle | shadow cast | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 20 deg (grazing) | 41 mm | swamps two rows behind it |
| **45 deg** | **15 mm** | **stays within its own row** |
| 70 deg | 5 mm | too flat, 0.3 mm features stop reading |

**Direction: from the FRONT (-Y) or BACK (+Y) edge of the tray — never from the ends.** Every family-A finger protrudes in **+X** (along the row) and the witness ledges protrude in -X:

* light from -Y or +Y: each finger is lit on one flank and throws a shadow sideways across its own pad, fully visible from straight above. **Correct.**
* light from +X (the way the fingers point): lit face-on, shadow falls behind the finger where the mast hides it. Useless.
* light from -X: the mast shadows its own fingers. Useless.

**Shoot each row twice — once lit from the front edge, once from the back.** Anything lost in shadow one way appears the other way.

### 0.2.2 Scoring — you do not have to do this by hand

**Route A (recommended): send the photographs and have them scored for you.** One frame per row, both light directions, pre-wash and post-wash. The tick-box tables below are a recording aid, not a task list you are obliged to complete.

**Route B: score at the bench.** It is simpler than the tables make it look. Every specimen is one tiny test that either survived printing or snapped off, and the whole measurement is which state it is in:

| mark | meaning |
|---|---|
| `FLAT` | present and straight — it survived |
| `CURL` | present but visibly curled or lifted — it yielded without breaking. This is the interesting one: it marks the transition and gives sub-rung resolution for free |
| `GONE` | nothing there — it failed |

That is the entire scoring vocabulary. Tick one box per row of the tables below, working down each tower.

Two things that will otherwise trip you up:

* **The little ledges are counting aids, not specimens.** Each mast carries a 0.20 mm witness ledge at every level, sized so it physically cannot fail. When several fingers in a row have snapped off, count ledges to work out which level the gap belongs to.
* **Height does not tell you size.** Masts alternate ascending (`U`) and descending (`D`), so a finger high up is not necessarily a long one. Always read `L` from the table, never from how far up it sits. This is deliberate — it is what keeps film fatigue and vat warming from correlating with the graded variable.

**Ballast columns are not specimens.** The square-based flaring columns carry no measurement and are deliberately unlabelled; they exist only to hold the per-layer cured area roughly constant so peel force does not drift with height. They have a **chamfered top** precisely so they can never be misread as a snapped-off specimen. Ignore them when scoring.

**There are visibly MORE of them than there used to be, and that is correct.** Deleting the row markers freed row-end space that the ballast solver immediately spent: plate 2 now carries 20 columns (was 4), plate 3 24 (was 18) and plate 4 17 (was 6). They are still not specimens.

**Reading the wall IDs on plate 4's towers.** Every family-A2 tower carries its ID a second time on the **-Y face of its mast**, 2.00 mm characters running UP the column, so the one photograph that shows all four shelves in profile also shows which tower you are looking at. Shoot along **+Y** (i.e. at the plate's FRONT face of the mast) and **rotate the photo 90 deg clockwise to read it** — the same rule the existing rotated pad labels already use. Free mnemonic: **the labelled side is the side on which the fingers point right.** Shooting the wrong side shows a *blank* mast, which is unmistakable rather than ambiguous — turn the part over and re-shoot; there is no failure mode that yields a WRONG ID. Light the wall labels with the lamp at the **upper end of the 40-50 deg band (45-50 deg)**: on a vertical face the shadow is `relief x tan(elevation)`, i.e. exactly anti-correlated with a +Z pad face, so at 40 deg the wall label's dark feature is the narrowest it gets (0.72 mm vs the pad's 1.04 mm) and at 45-50 deg it matches or beats it (0.80-0.90 vs 0.92-0.82 mm).

**Plate 2 only — the pad ID can be overhung by its own wing.** At e = 1.30 mm the surviving wing covers ~73 % of the ID glyph column seen from directly overhead. Shoot plate-2 rows **~15 deg off vertical from -X**, or read the ID after the wing has been removed. It is self-cancelling in the direction that counts: a cell whose wing has GONE — the interesting state — has a fully exposed label, and a survivor is placeable from its position in the monotone `w_n` ladder anyway.

## 0.3 What the plates can and cannot return

A pass/fail plate returns the **ratio** `p_eff / sigma_green` and its dependence on Y, on plan area, on direction and on cycle count. It cannot return a number in MPa for green strength, because p and sigma_green appear only as a quotient in every failure equation here. That ratio is what every engine gate actually consumes (`predicted stress < allowable`, both sides scaling together).

**Plate 1 has already returned one.** Family A survived every rung at every station, with corner sag only on the top two, so `L_crit >= 2.5920 mm` and therefore `p_eff / sigma_green <= 1.24e-04` — against 3.0e-4 assumed in `config.py` and 2.5e-3 in the active-layer LP. In pressure terms that is `p_eff <= 1.24 kPa` at sigma_g = 10 MPa, best estimate 0.6-1.2 kPa. Plates 2, 3 and 4 are re-centred on that: every ladder now brackets `p_eff in [0.4, 3] kPa` crossed with `sigma_g in [4.8, 10] MPa`.

**It returned a BOUND, not a value** — the ladder saturated at its top. That is what plate 4 exists to fix, and it is why plate 4 should be printed before any result from plates 2 and 3 is treated as absolute.

## Family A — flat-overhang ladder (single 0.050 mm layer, pure bending)

**What fails:** the FIRST layer of a finger is a 0.050 mm plate cantilevered its full length L and loaded by peel over its own area, so `sigma = 3 p L^2 / t^2`. If it goes, layers 2-6 have nothing to build on and the WHOLE finger is absent.

**What it looks like:** `FLAT` = finger present and straight. `CURL` = present but visibly curled or lifted (the layer yielded but did not fracture — this marks the transition and gives sub-rung resolution for free). `GONE` = nothing there.

**Counting aid:** each tower has a 0.20 mm witness ledge on its -X face at every level. The ledges cannot fail (3p(0.20)^2/(0.05)^2 = 0.14 MPa at 3 kPa) so the level scale stays countable after every finger above has gone.

**Read L from the table, NOT from the height.** L is deliberately NOT monotone in Z: towers alternate ascending (`U`) and descending (`D`) so that film fatigue, vat warming, resin ageing and debris — all of which climb with Z — are orthogonal to the graded variable instead of perfectly correlated with it.

## Family D — peel-direction control

**Why:** family A's fingers all point +X, so the whole length releases at one instant. A finger pointing **-Y** is released **tip-first** (the peel front reaches the tip before the root, so the still-adhered span shields the root); one pointing **+Y** is released **root-first** (the released span, and hence the moment arm, grows to the full L). Expect **+Y to fail at SHORTER L than -Y**. A null result — all three directions failing at the same level — is equally valuable: it means orientation need not enter the engine at all.

Lengths are family-A rungs, so D is anchored to A's absolute bracketing.

## Family B — necked cantilever (Craig's actual observed defect)

**What fails:** peel pulls the wing DOWN toward the film while the pad is held by the plate, so the neck is in TENSION plus BENDING, never compression. Because M = F·e is constant down the whole column, the smallest section governs: the prismatic neck. `sigma = (F/(d_n·w_n))·(1 + 6e/w_n)` with `F = p·A`, `A = 90.0 mm^2`, `d_n = 1.00 mm`.

**What it looks like:** `PASS` = the 11.25 x 8.00 x 5.00 mm wing sits on its neck. `FAIL` = pad + tapered spine + a bare neck stub, wing absent. `PART` = neck visibly cracked, torn or drawn out with the wing still attached and tilted — this marks the boundary cell. `CAT` = wing welded flat to the film / found as a blob; record separately and check its neighbours for a re-encounter scar.

**Stub shape tells you where it broke.** The neck has an identical 0.15 mm blend at BOTH ends. A stub that ends in a clean prismatic square = the neck failed (what you want). A stub that ends in a visible flare = the ramp above it failed and that cell is void, not data.

**The `e` sweep is a MOMENT-ARM EXPONENT TEST.** At the measured pressure pure tension is unreachable (it would need a 56 x 56 mm mass), so every cell here is bending-dominated. `e` doubles per column and the ladder ratio is sqrt(2), so the prediction is that the boundary shifts by EXACTLY +1.00 rung per column. A measured shift that is not 1.00 rung falsifies the `(1 + 6e/w)` form. Each column carries its own 8-rung window of the 10-rung master ladder, shifted +1 rung per column, so all three columns have identical guard margins.

**What fails:** the cap is concentric on the tip so there is no bending term at all — `sigma_t = 4·p·A_i/(pi·d_t^2)`. The minimum section, the intended fracture plane, is the truncation of the taper.

**What it looks like:** binary and visible from a metre — a mushroom cap either sits on its stalk or the cap is gone and only a pointed stalk remains. Note separately if the break surface is a **ring** part-way up the flare cone rather than a clean tip break: that invalidates the rung (the flare failed, not the tip).

**The tip is now PRISMATIC over 0.20 mm (4 layers).** Previously `d_t` existed at exactly one z, the slicer samples a layer at a finite offset from that plane, and the flare above it grows 8 mm of diameter per mm of z — so within half a layer the printed minimum section was up to +0.20 mm wider than nominal, which is x9 in area at the bottom rung and decreases monotonically up the ladder. That is a systematic that MIMICS the quantity being measured. With a prismatic tip at least three layers are sampled wholly inside the prism.

**Tip heights are staggered on purpose.** Each stack is raised by a different offset so the caps do not all land in one z band (which would put a large spike in the plate's per-layer area exactly where the tips are failing). ### Reading the GRADUATIONS on an A2 rung

Craig's request: *"if the tip fails, we can see how much did actually remain connected"*. Every A2 rung carries ruler ticks on its TOP face at a uniform **0.50 mm** pitch measured from the **ROOT** (the mast's +X face), with every 4th tick a **MAJOR**, so the majors land every 2.00 mm.

| | width along X | relief above the shelf | span across Y |
|---|---|---|---|
| minor | 0.10 mm | 0.15 mm (3 layers) | 1.96 mm (full width) |
| **major** | **0.20 mm** | **0.30 mm** (6 layers) | 1.96 mm (full width) |

Both run the **full width** of the rung, as asked. A major is twice a minor in BOTH of the other two channels, so it reads as a major in plan view and in profile silhouette alike.

**Turning a photograph into a number:**

1. Photograph the tower **in profile along +-Y** — the same frame that carries the mast's wall ID and all four shelves.
2. On the shelf of interest, count outward from the mast face: **M** = tall full-height ticks present (0-3), **m** = short ticks after the last major (0-3).
3. **n = 4M + m** is the index of the last tick present, so `L_survive >= 0.500 x n` mm.
4. Estimate the residual **r** from the last tick to the fracture, in units of the 0.500 mm pitch. Report `L_survive = 0.500 x (n + r)`.
5. If the tip is crisp and at least 0.20 mm past the last tick the rung is INTACT — score `FLAT` or `CURL` as usual. Otherwise score **`PARTIAL n+r`**; the stub is then a free extra ladder point at `sigma = 3 p L_survive^2 / t^2` with t = 0.050 mm.

**Which level am I looking at?** Read the shelf's Z, not the tick count. The `ticks` column in each tower's table below is the INTACT count; a rung that tore back shows fewer, and a torn long rung can read identically to an intact short one. (Over-counting is impossible, so a count HIGHER than the table allows does mean you are reading the wrong level.)

**One honest caveat.** The ticks bond to the shelf's TOP surface (finger layer 6); the measurand is the fracture of layer 1. If a shelf delaminates between layers rather than snapping cleanly, `PARTIAL n+r` reports where the TOP surface ended, which need not be where layer 1 failed. Flag any specimen whose break face looks stepped and layer-parallel rather than transverse.

**Free bonus — the profile photograph is now self-calibrating.** The pitch is exactly 0.500 mm, ON the specimen, IN its own focal plane: an exact scale bar, which kills the dominant systematic in macro photogrammetry (calibrating magnification from a ruler that is not in the object plane). Root-anchoring guarantees at least one tick survives on every rung, so every frame is calibrated, and the full-width ticks add an orthogonal Y scale of 1.96 mm from the same frame. An off-axis shot also audits itself: apparent pitch `p' = 0.500 cos(psi)`, so a 1 % pitch error means psi = 8.1 deg and the frame can be CORRECTED rather than discarded. Two caveats on that: sensitivity `dp'/dpsi` vanishes as psi -> 0 (resolving psi = 3 deg needs the pitch to 0.14 %), and it is structurally blind to rotation about X, which does not change the apparent pitch at all.

If you want the deflected SHAPE rather than just the tip: uniform pressure gives `delta/delta_tip = 0.354` at mid-span against `0.3125` for a tip point load, a separation of **4.15 % of delta_tip**. At `delta_tip = 0.5` mm that is 0.021 mm — about 5 px at 1:1 on APS-C and BELOW the noise floor of a phone macro. It is a 1:1-macro-only measurement, and it needs an INTACT tip (you must normalise by `delta_tip`), so it applies to the rungs BELOW `L_crit`, not to the torn ones the graduations exist for.


**Read L from the table, NOT from the height.** L is deliberately NOT monotone in Z: towers alternate ascending (`U`) and descending (`D`) so that film fatigue, vat warming, resin ageing and debris — all of which climb with Z — are orthogonal to the graded variable instead of perfectly correlated with it.

## Family E — thin-wall resolution AND cure-quality witness

**Two jobs.** (a) It converts every NOMINAL dimension on the other plates into an EFFECTIVE one: anti-aliasing is on (`edgeblurtype 2`, `mingreylevel 95`) so edge pixels are only partly cured and a nominal 0.25 mm neck does not print 0.25 mm wide. (b) It is the only **Y control** on the whole experiment. Tilt release, resin drainage, gap-opening rate and any thermal gradient in the vat are ALL monotone in machine Y and no passive coupon can separate them — but family E is loaded four orders of magnitude below any failure boundary, so whatever it does with Y is cure quality, not mechanics. **If E's boundary rung shifts with Y, the whole hinge fit on plate 1 is void.** Read E first.

**No strength confound, verified.** The walls are 1.50 mm tall (not 4 mm) and propped at both ends by 0.60 mm posts, giving an Euler buckling safety factor of 995 on the thinnest wall at 3 kPa. Whatever fails here failed to RESOLVE; it did not break.

**PLATE 1 RESULT: this gate PASSED.** Every wall printed full height at all five Y stations, including the thinnest at 0.10 mm (7.1 px in X). Two consequences carried into plates 2-4: there is no cure-quality gradient front to back, so the position comparison measures mechanics and not chemistry; and 0.10 mm is a CONFIRMED resolvable feature size and is the hard floor on every graded dimension. Family E is repeated unchanged on every plate as the same validity gate — re-read it first on each new plate.

## Family TIP — the load-law exponent (Q3) and the squeeze film (Q4)

A cap of area `A_cap` on a slender tip of diameter `d_t`. Under an AREA-pressure law the load on the tip goes as `A_cap`; under a LINE-tension law it goes as the cap PERIMETER; under a squeeze film it goes as `A_cap^2 / d^3`. The three predict different columns of this grid failing, and the grid is built so they cannot be confused: 24 cells covering the full 8 tip rungs x 3 cap diameters exactly once, `r(log d_t, log A_cap) = 0.000000`, and constant column sums (9 / 4 / 13) for rung, cap and stagger level.

The X column is the RELATIVE load each theory's discriminant puts on the tip; it spans 0.64 to 15625, a factor of 24000, which is what makes the exponent fittable from one plate.

**Score `F` if the cap is gone or hanging, `P` if the tip is bent or necked but holding, `-` if intact.** A cap found FUSED TO A CATCH POST voids that cell -- photograph it and strike it out.

## Plate 6 — what this plate is FOR

Plate 5 put the single-arm capacity boundary near `X = A_cap / A_arm ~= 25`, but on CONICAL support tips — the breaks were observed at the base of the cylindrical portion, wherever the cone happened to be thinnest, so the failing cross-section was never quite the nominal tip diameter, and the 2.5x ladder step could only say "somewhere near 25". Plate 6 re-measures the same boundary on CONSTANT-DIAMETER CYLINDERS, so the failing section is unambiguous and always equal to the nominal, stepping the ladder at ~1.25x instead of 2.5x (**family A6**). One further question rides the same build: does N support arms buy N times the capacity of one, and does the arm line's orientation to the peel front matter (**family B6**).

Each cap reaches full diameter through a **flare** at 0.20 mm per side per 0.050 mm layer — the rate plate 5's TIP family printed successfully — not through a single-layer step. That does not move the measured boundary: the arm cylinder is still the minimum section at every height on the load path, so peak arm stress is `p_eff * A_cap / A_arm` however the load gets there. It only shrinks the first-lamina cantilever, which is what made the thinnest-arm cells of the previous draft guaranteed failures for a reason that had nothing to do with capacity.

**Do not try to read anything from the break HEIGHT.** The arm is the minimum section everywhere, so the stub left on the plate is a bare full-length cylinder no matter when it broke; the informative truncated cone leaves with the cap and is lost in the vat. The reading is strictly binary.

**Family C6 (arm free length) was dropped.** The original design brief for this plate carried a third family varying `arm_l_free`; it did not survive to this build. `manifest.json` places only A6 (17) and B6 (17), and `verification.json`'s `design_identities.plate6_specimen_count` gate asserts exactly that: "34 placed (A6=17, B6=17, other=0); expected 34 = 17+17, no stray families (C6 must be gone)". Nothing on the plate carries a C6 ID — every A6 specimen uses the same fixed 6.00 mm arm free length, and free length is not a variable anywhere on this build.

Three rows, front to back, Y = -37.50 / 0.00 / +37.50 mm. **Every row carries both families mixed together** so family is never a function of Y — a boundary measured by A6 is directly comparable to B6's result without a front-to-back confound. Each row is split into two glued **trains**, each under the 78 mm wash-station ceiling — six trains total; see "Plate 6 — train and ballast structure" below for exactly which specimens are in which.

**Handling rider — read before touching the plate.** The owner scores after washing, drying and curing — never on the still-wet green part (see the scoring sheet for why). That means Family A6's thin-arm survivors, down to a 0.65 mm arm at roughly 3 g of break force, must survive the wash basket intact for the reading to mean anything: a wash-basket loss reads as a false failure at precisely the boundary the plate exists to measure. Handle post-wash trains gently, one at a time, and check the wash basket itself for any detached caps before concluding a count.

**Reading the IDs.** Every specimen's ID is embossed on the front apron facing you (-Y), sitting entirely BELOW the apron's crenellated top edge so no glyph is ever left hanging off a notch. That crenellation is also the scale bar: 1.00 mm pitch, every 5th notch cut deeper.

## Family B6 — multi-arm load sharing (does N arms buy N x?)

Every cell carries the SAME 30.00 mm^2 cap. The strip cells put it on N constant-diameter 0.798 mm arms in a line, N laddered against two arm pitches — 2.000 mm (`T`) and 2.800 mm (`W`) — with the arm line's ORIENTATION to the peel front (which sweeps -Y to +Y) crossed in on the cells that discriminate the mechanism. `M1`-`M4` put the same cap area on ONE round arm, laddered in arm diameter, as the matched single-arm calibration. `X_tot = A_cap / (N * A_arm)` is what N arms sharing the load evenly would see; `X_arm = A_cap / A_arm` is what ONE arm alone would see if the cap does not share load between arms at all (the ZIPPER failure). Every strip arm sees `X_arm = 60.15`, which is `M1`'s own `X_tot` — so the zipper prediction is not a model output, it is "M1 fails, therefore every strip cell must fail too".

**THE HEADLINE — `3TY` vs `M3`, replicated at N=4 by `4TY` vs `M4`.** Matched cap area and matched TOTAL arm cross-section, split across 3 (resp. 4) arms versus 1. If sharing is additive the strip survives where the single arm does not; if it is a zipper they fail together. `3TY`/`3TX` and `3WY`/`3WX` (and their `2` replicates) are the orientation contrast at each pitch: same cap, same N, same arm, rotated 90 degrees. `PB` is a BLIND positive control (`3TY`'s footprint on 0.247 mm arms, `X_tot` = 209) and MUST FAIL; do not tell the scorer which cell it is before scoring.

**Any PARTIAL cap on ANY B6 specimen is scored VOID, never a survival** — especially on the headline contrast, whose entire value depends on a clean present/gone read.

`train`/`pos` is the physical train (see the train table further down) and the specimen's counted position within it, left to right, matching the scoring sheet. `X_tot = A_cap / (N * A_arm)` is what N arms sharing load evenly would see; `X_arm = A_cap / A_arm` is what one arm alone would see (the zipper prediction) — for `M1`-`M4` (N=1) the two are identical. `x (mm)` is the specimen's actual pixel-snapped build position, straight from `manifest.json`.
