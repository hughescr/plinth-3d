## 1. The biggest lessons

**L1. The product never reached the printer, as far as any record shows (F1).** In 100 days and 2,098 commits, no record exists of a print
of a miniature supported by plinth (absence of a record; ask Craig, Q1). All the real print
data is from calibration coupons (plates 1, 4, 5, 6, H2: unsupported shelves and caps on arms, no miniature; the coupon geometry was
generated by plinth-era plate-generator code and printed) and Craig's own hand
supporting in ChituBox. Everything labelled "field feedback" after 2026-08-04 is Craig looking at a file on screen. The tool was tuned to
his eye, not to the printer, and the gap between the two was never measured. The new project must put a physical print at the end of
the first slice, side by side with a hand-ChituBox version of the same model, and treat print outcomes as the acceptance signal.

**L2. Hard limits must be inputs at the step that creates the quantity.** Plinth made contact size an OUTPUT: reach pitch, then Voronoi
load, then radius, then about 15 later passes that moved and grew contacts. The 0.8 mm cap and no-fusion rule were then bolted on: pcap 1-8,
CBT (0 of 88 fixes feasible), cr1, cpa1-6. Round_50, "the simplest possible support job", went from 198 arms to 466 (the physics needs
~223; that figure is a model-derived bound, not a measurement) and from 10.7 s to 102 s, and still failed. ChituBox appears to take the contact
as INPUT: a fixed sphere along the face normal at fixed spacing (`E/perf-2026-10-14/validator/RETHINK.md` 1.1, `CAP-CONVERGENCE.md`,
`mem/limits-are-construction-rules-2026-10-28`). The claim that its output "meets its limits by construction" is UNVERIFIED (F2): the
profile settings in the cfgx are not the placement algorithm, and `RETHINK-CHALLENGE.md` treats normal stubs as candidates, not guarantees.
The rule, in Craig's words: size from load, never above the cap; at the cap, add more non-touching supports. The design lesson stands
(build the limit into the step that creates the quantity); whether ChituBox itself satisfies it is a thing to measure on its native files
(`test-data/chitubox-native/`).

**L3. Only the written file and "would Craig print this?" count.** Every proxy lied at least once. "24 of 37 emit" when about 0 would
print, and 4 of the 24 were display bases. A "0.96 mm neck" came from planned parameters when the file had no neck. A lane's "0.8 mm cap"
left 1.70 and 2.19 mm contacts in the file, and the orchestrator relayed that to Craig. "FU 0" counted only fusion at the model surface while
tips sat inside other supports. Fixtures that passed the project's own parser all failed in ChituBox. What worked was an independent reader
of the emitted bytes that shares no types with the engine (`R/tools/plinth-audit`). Every number needs what it counts, what it does not
count, its denominator and its source file (`mem/measure-from-emitted-file`, `mem/stop-and-review-2026-10-29`).

**L4. The physics is thin; the owner's practice is rich, and plinth overrode his practice with thin physics.** Solid numbers: snap ratio
X* about 25 (cone 25.00-25.34, cylinder 19.9-30.5), from one resin, one cap area, n=1 at the bracket edges, with print date and ambient
not recorded. Flat shelf reach: survived to 2.13 mm, all failed by 4.67 mm. 0.10 mm walls resolved (smaller was not tested, so 0.10 mm is
the smallest tested wall, not a universal minimum feature, F5). Load sharing was at least additive in the tested geometry up to N=6 (no
upper bracket was found for strips; the ~20% fin-root bonus is a confound, F8). Plate 5's print conditions were not recorded; plate 6
printed 2026-07-30, scored 2026-07-31, about 30 C, black resin with no vendor recorded.
Failure is at the narrowest section just above the contact. Everything else (45 deg overhang angle, slenderness limit, brace loads, neck
bending, peel pressure) is convention, literature or a placeholder. 273 of 306 registry constants were placeholders, and the registry typed
owner rulings and literature values as `Measured`. Plinth placed tips on a conservative 2.13 mm reach from one cantilever coupon, giving
1-4 mm spacing, while Craig's working ChituBox profiles use 4-8 mm. Then it was surprised to be over-supported (08-31, 09-30). The PRD must
store owner practice as the baseline and physics as bracketed, replaceable parameters. A departure from his practice needs a measured
reason.

**L5. The process built the architecture.** Lanes were told "add X behind a flag, byte-identical when off". That gave 444 k lines of Rust,
three engine generations compiled side by side (the CLI default was still the legacy engine nobody ran), 407 `PLINTH_*` knobs, 93 policy
fields, an 8,288-line `lib.rs`, and 20+ passes mutating one shared `Vec<SweepStrut>`. A guarantee checked by an early pass was re-broken by a
later one (a clearance of 0.318 mm measured early was 0.067 mm after growth). Rust did not cause this. The reward structure did, and Rust
made 14-argument pass functions painless (lessons-tech 1.4, 1.8, 5).

**L6. The loop failed on method, not compile speed.** Incremental builds took 3-15 s. Over 57.6 h, 55% of the wall time had a job running
and no agent thinking. The median edit-to-model-file time was 32 min, and about 25 h went to lock-waits in 2 days. Narrow lanes stopped at
the first miss and were respawned: 52 worktrees (519 GB, 41 untouched for 14 days), 58 `for-craig*` folders, 55 agents in one session. The
orchestrator recorded Craig's rulings as memory notes but did not change the design. Eight anger events each produced a new brief rule, not
a new method. A read-only rethink by a model that had not built the code found the root cause in one pass, after eight build lanes had not
(lessons-process 1, 2).

## 4. Open questions for the owner interview (30)

Rules for the interview: every question has an answer the PRD can use (a choice, a file, a photo, a list or a number). "Unknown" and "I will check" are valid answers; record them as unknown, never fill a gap with the old project's number. Ask for files, photos, setup and edits wherever the answer is evidence. Section 0 and 2 state what the old project believed; do not read them to Craig as facts.

**A. Facts first (what exists, before any design)**
1. Has any file generated by plinth (a miniature with plinth's supports, not a calibration plate) ever been printed? Answer yes (which file, which printer settings, what failed, what you edited first), no, or unknown. If yes, send the file, photos and the setup (resin lot, ChituBox profile, film, tank temperature).
2. Do you still have any of these, and where: the H2 plate file `H2-exact-grounded-full-plate.chitubox` and its printed parts (the break surfaces and the detached heads), the original photos of plates 1, 4, 5, 6 (the Photos library holds HEIC originals; only small copies survive in `reference/experiments/plate-photos/`), the plate 5 and 6 slicer profile exports? And did ChituBox write `cylinder-cube-sphere.ctp` (yes, no, unknown)?
3. Do the supports ChituBox auto-generates with your profile (which ChituBox version, which profile) print these minis reliably as generated? Answer reliably, usually after edits, or no, and list what you change by hand (where on the model, what kind of change).

**B. Goal and scope**
4. Is v1 done when you print its supports unedited, or when editing them in ChituBox is faster than rebuilding them by hand? Choose one, or state both with an order.
5. First target class: Round 25/50/100 bases first, then which mini (Deinonychus, a skeleton, Giant Bat)? Give the order for the first three.
6. Is hollowing in v1, or after unhollowed minis print?
7. Which later stages are in scope (plate packing, an own slicer, sending to the printer over the network), and in what order? "None" is allowed.
8. Is v1 one printer (Saturn 4 Ultra 16K) and one resin (Sunlu Water-Wash ABS-Like Black, 0.05 mm layers)? Answer yes, or name each addition.
9. **C. Physical truth and acceptance**
10. Acceptance lifecycle: for each step from slicing to the finished mini, state the pass/fail test you use: slice preview, detail kept, print, support removal, wash, cure, drain, handling. Leave a step blank if you do not test it.
11. Scar standard by face: for the face, the front of the body, the base underside and hidden surfaces, what is the largest mark you accept (a size, or mark it on a printed or on-screen example)?
12. Removal: which tools do you use, and how many minutes of cleanup per mini do you accept for a small mini and for a Large one?
13. Trapped resin and damage: is any trapped resin in a hollowed mini a failure; and at which moments do you check for damage (off the plate, after wash, after cure, after handling)?
14. The 0.8 mm limit (the 10-28 ruling, a construction rule, stands unless you change it): how should the mark be measured on an irregular contact (diameter of the patch on the surface, or an equivalent area)? Is it there because a larger mark fails the print, costs cleanup, or shows? Which surfaces need a stricter limit?
15. Tool access: show, on a real or on-screen example, the closest two contact marks that still let your tool in. A picture or a marked model, not a number.
16. Which uncertainty blocks your next real print, and how many calibration plates or print runs are you willing to spend before the first miniature print? "Zero" is allowed; then state which numbers you accept from your ChituBox practice.
17. Will the first slice's output be printed beside a hand-supported ChituBox version of the same model, as the acceptance test? How many print runs per week can the project use?
18. Of the job manifest (`reference/models-and-jobs/job-manifest.md`), how many minis must print with no lost parts for the project to count as done?

**D. Workflow**
19. Do you review each output in ChituBox, or a rendered picture first and ChituBox only for candidates? If pictures, name the view you want (ChituBox's own view and colours).
