# Owner interview — round 2 (2026-10-29)

1. **Pose: plinth chooses it.** Reasons: put complex detail "up", away from the plate, so supports don't collide with or scar it; place drain holes so large voids drain without destroying important detail. Manual habit: orient "frilly" elements mostly vertical (self-supporting); minis "face up" so supports don't land on faces/fronts. He admits on-table, painted minis rarely show small scars, "but it still *feels* like I should" — treat as a preference with low weight against print success.
2. **Hollowing is in v1.** Hollowing itself is straightforward; drain-hole location and orientation are the tricky part and must be decided together (drains depend on pose), not retrofitted.
3. **Resins (by frequency).** Nova3D Water Washable ABS-like tough (gray); Sunlu Water Washable ABS (black); Anycubic Water Washable ABS-like 3.0 (clear, for translucent parts). Performance similar across them in his experience. All are hand-painted with acrylics afterwards. (Layer height not stated — ask/take from profile.)
4. **How he judges a print, in priority order.**
   1. It printed: nothing fell off the supports, no "smooshed" unsupported regions, no sag, no delamination.
   2. Easy support removal: not packed too densely; not attached too solidly.
   3. Resin used (less is cheaper, all else equal) — minor.
   4. Print time (minor; essentially total height for a given pose). Variable layer height/exposure is "way down the list".
5. **Scars.** Hidden/underneath scars are irrelevant unless they harm integrity (e.g. a hole through a base that shows on the visible side). Small scars mostly vanish under paint. What matters: big chunks out of a detailed face, or supports that knobble a thin blade — these slow removal and make it delicate, risking breakage (sword blades, fingers) and reprints.
6. **Removal tools.** Small wire snips; two long thin steel manicure tools (one hooked tip, one tapered point) — photo `removal-tools.png`; a Fantik F2 rotary tool for drilling/grinding/smoothing; fingers when easy.

Notes for the PRD: success hierarchy = prints > removable without damage > resin > time. Thin, fragile features (blades, fingers) are the critical places for contact choice — removal force near them must be low. Clearance between contacts must admit snips and a pointed/hooked pick.
