# §0. WHAT THE WALL IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

At `bfa5b3b` the Weeping Treant clears `initial::build_initial_contacts` for the first time
(≈267 s) and then spends every remaining second of the 600 s timebox inside
`PreparedSupportRun::route_initial_cco`, hashing. It is not computing geometry, not querying the
BVH, not refusing a cap, and not leaking. It is re-hashing structures it has already hashed. This
design measures exactly how much: **≈843 GB of byte-at-a-time FNV-1a over a full 553-entry
routing cadence, of which ≈99.7 % is provably redundant** (§M4). The remedy is two changes of
different character — one value-preserving hoist that needs no permission, and one
value-**changing** accumulator restructure that needs an owner ruling — and the second is
unavoidable for algebraic reasons proved in §F5.

# §F. FINDINGS

## F1 — What `CcoController::digest` digests, per what unit, how often

**Per unit: once per cadence terminal, unconditionally, as the first statement of the growth call.**

- The routing loop is a plain `for terminal in cadence` at `crates/plinth-support/src/runner.rs:1650–1685`,
  over a cadence built at `runner.rs:1648–1649` (`specialized_terminals()` then `regular_terminals()`).
- Every non-quarantined terminal calls `controller.grow_terminal_with_finalized_anchor(...)`
  (`runner.rs:1666`), whose **first statement** is
  `let controller_digest_before = self.digest();` — `crates/plinth-support/src/cco.rs:1397`.
- Every **quarantined** terminal calls `advance_skipped_terminal` (`runner.rs:1656`), which also
  calls `self.digest()` — `cco.rs:1488`.
- **So `digest()` is called exactly once per cadence entry, on both arms.** Confirmed by
  measurement, not inference: the instrumented counter `ctrl_calls` equals `inserted` at every one
  of the 209 logged terminals (§M2). There is no third production call inside the loop.
- One further call at seal: `cco.rs:1311`, `let final_controller_digest = self.digest();` inside
  `finalize_transcript`. Two more outside the stage: `runner.rs:2763` (brace preflight) and
  `runner.rs:2832` (rewire preflight). Those three are **O(state) once**, not per item, and F6
  shows they are load-bearing.

**What it walks (`cco.rs:1775–1864`).** It is *not* the cached `const fn` at `cco.rs:863`. It is a
complete walk of accumulated controller state, in this word order:

| # | segment | source | size at step *k* |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `inserted`, `terminals.len()`, `terminal_cadence.len()` | `cco.rs:1783–1785` | 3 words |
| 2 | every entry of `terminal_cadence` | `cco.rs:1786–1788` | *k* words |
| 3 | `sealed` | `cco.rs:1789` | 1 word |
| 4 | every record in `terminals` (a `BTreeMap<u32, TerminalCadenceRecord>`, `cco.rs:1192`), in **terminal-id order** | `cco.rs:1790–1851` | *k* records |
| 4a | …each `Routed` record's route evidence + winner | `cco.rs:1793–1800`, `digest_route_evidence` `cco.rs:1867–1877` | 9 + 4 words |
| 4b | …each `SkippedQuarantined` record | `cco.rs:1801–1813` | 6 words |
| 4c | …each `UnroutedNoWinner` record's **entire** `anchor_seat_refusals` list, five words per refusal, and its `anchor_refusals` list | `cco.rs:1814–1847`; `digest_anchor_seat_refusals` `cco.rs:2128–2149` | **5 · Rₖ words** |
| 5 | the whole host kd-tree: `hosts.indexed_members` and `hosts.samples` | `cco.rs:1852–1864` | 1 + 2·H_m + 1 + 5·H_s words |

**Complexity.** `Θ(N²) + Θ(N·R) + Θ(N·H)` for *N* cadence terminals, *R* total accumulated
refusals, *H* host samples — exactly as the H6 record derived (`CURRENT-STATUS.md:4950–4962`).
Measurement (§M2) says the `Θ(N·R)` term is not merely dominant, it is essentially the whole
thing: **at cadence entry 282 a single `digest()` call hashes 192,168,908 words = 1,537 MiB**,
against 5·R = 193,960,920 — i.e. **the refusal lists *are* the digest**, to within a rounding error.

## F3 — What the digest EXISTS to guarantee — and it is never compared

`controller_digest_before` is:

- **stored** in `TerminalRouteEvidence` (`cco.rs:635`, written at `cco.rs:1436`/`:1449` via
  `route_evidence` `cco.rs:2237–2253`) and in `TerminalDisposition::SkippedQuarantined`
  (`cco.rs:1488`);
- **copied forward** into the sealed transcript row (`cco.rs:1978`, `FinalizedSkippedEvidence`);
- **re-hashed** into subsequent `digest()` calls (`cco.rs:1812`, `cco.rs:1876`) and into
  `transcript_rows_digest` (`cco.rs:2038`, `cco.rs:2030`);
- **never compared against anything.** `finalize_transcript_row`'s `checked_route` closure
  (`cco.rs:1917–1926`) validates `physical_contact_id`, `terminal_id`, `original_key`,
  `plan_digest`, `loads_digest`, `geometry_digest` — and deliberately **not**
  `controller_digest_before` or `forest_digest_before`. A repo-wide grep for
  `controller_digest_before` (24 hits) finds no equality test outside `#[cfg(test)]`.

**So its guarantee is not a tripwire; it is a hash-chain link.** Each record binds the entire
prior history of the controller, so a transcript row cannot be forged, reordered or silently
dropped without moving `final_controller_digest`, `transcript_digest` and therefore
`snapshot_digest`. It is provenance/attestation, and it is
exactly the job `contact_attestation_digest` already does **in the same struct, with an O(1)
chained accumulator**:

> **A citation the brief asked for, and which does not exist — recorded rather than propagated.**
> The task named "the CCO winner-commit tripwire from **OQ-11**". OQ-11 is not that. It is in
> `docs/rust-migration/contact-physics-2026-08-04.md:394`, `:477`, `:947`, and it asked whether an
> unreproducible contact figure should be re-measured or struck — **ruled: struck**. It has no
> bearing on `CcoController::digest`. The winner-commit discipline that *does* live near this code
> is **PR-8/OD-9 (F3)**, the seat-attestation chain (`cco.rs:1198–1205`, `runner.rs:1677–1682`,
> `runner.rs:1689–1698`), and that is the one cited here. No design decision below rests on OQ-11.

## F4 — Where the value goes, and therefore what a change to it costs

`controller_digest_before` → transcript rows → `transcript_rows_digest` (`cco.rs:2007`) →
`ordered_disposition_digest`; and `final_controller_digest` (`cco.rs:1311`, `:1320`) →
`finalized_transcript_digest` (`cco.rs:2092`) → `FinalizedCcoTranscript::digest()` →
`SupportVerificationSnapshot.transcript_digest` (`runner.rs:2557`) →
`verification_snapshot_digest` (`runner.rs:3443–3462`) → **`snapshot_digest`**.

**Therefore: any change to the controller digest moves all seven frozen OD-8 snapshot digests.**
That is not a reason not to do it — OD-8 (`CURRENT-STATUS.md:306`, the owner's *"figure out what
the correct process/math/physics should be and implement that"*) explicitly lifted
behavior-preservation and replaced it with *"Silent geometry change remains forbidden: every
behavioral difference must be measured, reported, and physics-justified."* It is a reason the
change must go through the FU-10 re-pin protocol (§D2.4).

## F5 — Bit-for-bit incrementality is **algebraically impossible** here. This is provable, not a judgement call

The digest is FNV-1a: `h ← (h ⊕ b) · p` over `u64` (`cco.rs:1776–1782`). Two independent obstructions:

1. **FNV-1a admits no composition law.** The step mixes GF(2)⁶⁴ addition (XOR) with Z/2⁶⁴
   multiplication. There is no ring in which both are the same operation, so there is no function
   `⊕̂` with `F(h, S₁‖S₂) = F(h,S₁) ⊕̂ F(∅,S₂)`. A "rolling hash" that combines a retained tail
   hash with a new prefix hash **does not exist for this algorithm**.
2. **Even a retained-prefix scheme fails, because the prefix itself changes every call.** Segment 1
   of the stream is `(inserted, |terminals|, |cadence|)` = `(k, k, k)` — it takes a **new value at
   every step**, at the **front** of the stream. Every retained fold state downstream of it is
   invalidated. And segment 4 iterates a `BTreeMap` in **terminal-id order** while insertions
   arrive in **cadence order**, so a new record generally splices into the middle of the stream
   rather than appending to its end — a second, independent reason retention cannot help.

Reordering the stream so that the volatile-small parts sit at the end would fix (2) — and would
itself change the value. **There is no value-preserving fix for the `Θ(N·R)` term.** Recommendation:
stop looking, and take the ruling.

*(The `Θ(N²)` cadence term is genuinely cheap and can stay exactly as it is: 553²/2 ≈ 153 k word
folds ≈ 1.2 MB over the whole run. It is not worth a line of code.)*

## F6 — The constraint a fix must not break: `digest()` must stay a **pure function of state**

`runner.rs:2763` (brace stage entry) and `runner.rs:2832` (rewire stage entry) both assert
`transcript.final_controller_digest() != controller.digest()` — a **recompute-from-state and
compare** against the sealed value. That is a real tamper tripwire and it is the only thing
standing between a mutated controller and two downstream stages.

**This kills the naïve chained-accumulator fix.** If `digest()` becomes a stored field maintained
by history, those two comparisons degrade into `field == field` and the tripwire silently loses its
teeth — a guarantee regression dressed as a speed-up, and precisely the failure mode the G2 record
warns about (*"Verify once, thread the proof — never stop verifying."* `perception.rs:4066–4075`).

The design therefore requires a **state-pure incremental** shape (§D2), not a chain: accumulators
that are maintained in O(delta) inside the loop **and** rebuildable from scratch in O(state) at the
three sites that need a real recompute.

## F7 — Memory: the +4.8 MiB/s is the transcript, and it is *one specific vector*

Measured (§M3): RSS at the Preparation→Routing boundary 2,252 MiB, whole-process peak
**3,704 MiB** — **+1,452 MiB while `R_seat` grew 0 → 50,216,402**, i.e. **≈30.3 bytes per retained
anchor-seat refusal**, which matches `AnchorSeatRefusal` (`anchor.rs:812–819`: `usize` +
`u32` + a fieldless enum + `Option<Finite>` ≈ 32 B with padding). **It is not churn.** It is
`NoWinnerEvidence::*::anchor_seat_refusals` (`cco.rs:1818–1845`), retained verbatim, one entry per
refused anchor-pool row per no-winner terminal.

Projection to a full 553-entry cadence: R_final ≈ 70 M refusals ≈ **2.1 GiB of refusal vectors**,
whole-process peak ≈ **4.3 GiB — under 5 % of the 90 GB watchdog arm.** Memory binds nothing,
before or after the fix, and the design proposes no memory change. **The measured 3,704 MiB peak
reproduces H6's 3,693 / 3,721 MiB to within 0.3 %** — the independent check that this scratch run
is measuring the same phenomenon H6 sampled.

**Adjacent finding, recorded but NOT proposed as work (see OQ-4):** a single no-candidate terminal
can retain a refusal list of **≈362,000 entries** — one per exterior face of the mesh (pool rows =
364,223). The step changes in `R_seat` are visibly quantised at ≈362 k (§M2). Evidence proportional
to the *pool* rather than to the *decision* is a legitimate design question, but changing it
changes transcript content and hence every digest, and it is not FU-32's to decide.

## F8 — Charging: routing is **completely uncharged**. This is the FU-8b shape, exactly

`route_initial_cco`'s only preflight is `validate_initial_cco_preflight` (`runner.rs:1738–1800+`),
which is **entirely digest and binding validation** — `plan.validate_binding`, four digest
comparisons, a cadence set check. It contains **no `Limit`, no `required`/`limit` pair, no counter,
no byte bound, and no clock.** A grep for the `resource:` charge idiom across `crates/plinth-support/src`
returns hits only in `initial.rs`, `brace.rs`, `coverage_measurement.rs` and
`transit_measurement.rs` — **nothing in `cco.rs`, and nothing in the routing half of `runner.rs`.**
Neither `SupportRoutingError` nor `CcoError` has a limit-shaped variant.

The FU-8b lesson applies verbatim (`support-resource-caps-2026-08-05.md:255–259`; ledger row
`CURRENT-STATUS.md:5217`): *"Both parts are **uncharged**, so **neither is a cap to raise** — a cap
cannot be too tight for work it never prices, and the remedy removes work rather than admitting
more."* A 600 s wall inside an uncharged loop is a **missing charge**, not a slow success. §D3
adds the honest charge, and it must be added **after** the work is removed, so the charge prices
the loop that will actually run.

**Wider, and worth the orchestrator's attention:** there is **no wall-clock deadline or work budget
anywhere in `plinth-support`** for routing, rewire or the seal. `REWIRE_WORK_BUDGET` is
`TrialSetting::disabled()` (`crates/plinth-core/src/constants/placeholders.rs:1334–1335`), so
`rewire::optimize` (`rewire.rs:202–217`) is an **unbounded loop**. The 600 s box is an external
`SIGKILL`, not an engine feature.

# §D. THE DESIGN

Two workstreams of **different authorization class**. They are independent and J1 can land alone.

## D1 — Workstream A (**value-preserving**): hoist the immutable authority out of the loop

**Shape: verify-once-then-thread-the-proof — the G2 precedent, applied verbatim.**

The G2 hoist (`CURRENT-STATUS.md:2226`, commits `4a8cc9b`, `36d1a63`) built
`VerifiedMeshFingerprint<'mesh>` (`perception.rs:4095`) and threaded it, with two properties this
design copies exactly:

1. **The subject travels inside the proof** (`perception.rs:4087–4094`) so a proof computed from
   one object cannot be presented alongside another — *"by construction rather than by discipline."*
2. **The staleness comparison stays at every site** (`perception.rs:4066–4075`) — *"This type
   hoists the hash, not the check… Verify once, thread the proof — never stop verifying."*

**Proposed token** (name and exact fields are an implementation call; the shape is not):

```rust
pub(crate) struct VerifiedRoutingAuthority<'a> {
    plan:    &'a SpecializedContactPlan,
    loads:   &'a FinalizedContactLoads,
    pool:    &'a SurfaceAnchorPool<'a>,
    geometry:&'a AnchorGeometryAuthority<'a>,
    plan_digest: u64,
    loads_digest: u64,
    pool_digest: u64,
    geometry_fingerprint: GeometryFingerprint,
    contact_by_id: BTreeMap<u64, usize>,   // kills the two O(|contacts|) linear finds
    contact_ids: Vec<u64>,                 // built once, not per terminal
}
```

Constructed **once** before `runner.rs:1650` by doing exactly what `bind` does today; threaded
through `FinalizedAnchorTerminalRoute`. Then:

- `bind` (`cco.rs:2194`) and `validate_replay` (`cco.rs:2217`) keep **every comparison they have
  today** and compare against the token's fields instead of re-hashing.
- `SurfaceAnchorPool::validate_against` (`anchor.rs:358`) keeps its pointer-identity and
  fingerprint arms (`anchor.rs:362`) — which are O(1) — and takes the recomputed
  `surface_anchor_pool_digest` arm (`anchor.rs:366–370`) from the token.
- `discover_finalized_anchor_seats`'s own `pool.validate_against(geometry)` (`anchor.rs:929`) does
  the same.
- `route.geometry.fingerprint()` (`anchor.rs:216`, three `String` clones per call) is read from the
  token.
- `runner.rs:1661–1665`'s linear `find` becomes a map lookup.

**Why this is sound, and it is not an argument from convenience.** `plan`, `loads`, `pool` and
`geometry` are behind shared references for the whole loop (`runner.rs:1670–1676`) and none of
their types has interior mutability (F2). The compiler already proves the property the re-hash was
re-checking. Anything *mutable* — the forest — keeps its per-terminal checks unchanged:
`request.validate(forest)` (`cco.rs:2222`), `forest.digest()` (`cco.rs:1398`),
`validate_finalized_anchor_route`'s rebind-and-compare (`cco.rs:2230–2233`), and
`route.config != AnchorSearchConfig::current()?` (`cco.rs:2287`) all stay.

**Not moved, deliberately:** the *values* recorded in the transcript. `plan_digest`, `loads_digest`,
`geometry_digest` in every `TerminalRouteEvidence` are the same u64s, computed from the same
structures by the same functions — just once. **All seven OD-8 digests must be byte-identical, and
that is J1's correctness claim in full.**

**Expected effect (M4):** ≈66 GB → ≈0.04 GB on the treant; Cylinder's 5,736 ms routing → ≈300 ms.

## D2 — Workstream B (**value-changing**): state-pure incremental accumulators for the controller digest

### D2.1 The shape

Keep `digest()` a **pure function of controller state** (F6 forbids a chain), but restructure its
word stream so the two expensive segments enter through accumulators that are maintained in
O(delta) and rebuildable in O(state):

- `records_acc: u64` — a **commutative** combination (`wrapping_add`, or XOR) of per-record hashes
  `h(record) = FNV(cadence_index ‖ terminal_id ‖ record_words)`. Each record's own hash is computed
  **once**, in `finish_terminal` (`cco.rs:1761–1771`), over that record's own refusal list only.
  Commutativity makes the `BTreeMap` iteration order irrelevant, which is precisely the obstruction
  F5(2) identified; **order is still bound**, twice over — by the `cadence_index` inside each
  record hash, and by the `terminal_cadence` vector, which stays hashed in full.
- `hosts_acc: u64` — FNV over `hosts.indexed_members` + `hosts.samples`, recomputed **only in the
  `KDTREE_REBUILD_EVERY = 25` branch** (`cco.rs:38`, `cco.rs:1768–1770`), because that is the only
  place hosts change.
- `digest()` becomes: `FNV(inserted, |terminals|, |cadence|, cadence…, sealed, records_acc, hosts_acc)`
  — `Θ(k)` cheap words per call, ≈1.2 MB over the whole run (F5).

### D2.2 Why this keeps F6's tripwire

Add `fn rebuild_accumulators(&self) -> (u64, u64)` that recomputes `records_acc` and `hosts_acc`
from scratch, and call it — **not** the cached fields — from the three sites that need a real
recompute: `finalize_transcript` (`cco.rs:1311`) and the two stage-boundary comparisons
(`runner.rs:2763`, `runner.rs:2832`). Cost: `O(state)` **three times per run**, versus `N` times
today. The tripwires keep every tooth they have; a mutated controller still fails them.

That is the same severance G3 made between *charged* and *executed*
(`CURRENT-STATUS.md:2346`): the digest is maintained incrementally where it is consumed as
evidence, and verified exhaustively where it is consumed as a check.

### D2.3 Complexity, after

| term | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| refusals | `Θ(N·R)` — ≈777 GB | `Θ(R)` — each refusal hashed once — ≈2.8 GB |
| cadence | `Θ(N²)` cheap | `Θ(N²)` cheap, unchanged (1.2 MB — not worth touching) |
| hosts | `Θ(N·H)` | `Θ(N/25 · H)` |

### D2.4 The price: all seven frozen OD-8 digests move, and that needs the FU-10 protocol

Per F4, the controller digest reaches `snapshot_digest`. OD-8 permits this; it forbids doing it
silently. The protocol is the one FU-10 executed (`CURRENT-STATUS.md:3687` `{#fu10-repin-2026-08-08}`):

1. **STOP-AND-REPORT** before re-pinning anything
   (`slicer-union-design-2026-08-07.md:589`, `:666` — *"No golden is re-pinned in this commit, and
   no test expectation was relaxed to accommodate the movement."*).
2. **Full attribution first** — `CURRENT-STATUS.md:620`: *"a golden is not re-pinned until every
   missing member is attributed."* Here attribution is unusually easy and unusually strong, and
   this is the design's best argument: **the controller digest is an input to no decision.** F3
   proved it is never compared. So the attribution claim is *"every observable except the three
   chained digest fields is bit-identical"* — provable directly (§D2.5).
3. **An explicit owner ruling** on the from→to table.
4. **Re-measure at the merged tip, record by append, sweep every downstream golden** — a from→to
   table for the seven snapshot digests plus any transcript/route goldens, with the append-only
   rule (`CURRENT-STATUS.md:3697`: *"Everything written before this section stands unamended."*).

### D2.5 The equivalence claim, stated so it can be tested

> For every corpus model, routing under J2 produces a transcript whose every row is
> `to_bits`-identical to the J1 tip's, **field by field, except** `route.controller_digest_before`
> and `skipped.controller_digest_before`; the final forest digest, `routed_contacts`,
> `final_live_members`, `plan_digest`, `loads_digest`, `geometry_digest`,
> `ordered_disposition_digest`'s non-controller inputs, and every `NoWinnerEvidence` payload are
> **unchanged**; and `digest()` recomputed by `rebuild_accumulators` equals `digest()` maintained
> incrementally, at every step, on every model.

This is the FU-8c/G6a shape: **retain the old walk as the `#[cfg(test)]` oracle** and compare
against it, exactly as `first_safe_proposal` (G3) and `reference_contacts_x_rows_scan` (G6) were
retained.

## D3 — The charge (F8's debt)

Add, in J4 and **only after J1+J2 have removed the redundant work**, a counted charge for routing
in the `initial::preflight` idiom (`initial.rs:1017`, `:1044`, receipt `charge_covers_execution`
`initial.rs:992–1012`, typed drift refusal `initial.rs:305`):

- **Charge:** `cadence_len × pool_rows` seat evaluations (the real Θ(N·P) search that remains once
  the hashing is gone: 553 × 364,223 ≈ **201 M** on the treant) plus the retained-refusal row count,
  against a **new, registered, honestly-derived** limit.
- **Meter beside the charge, never instead of it** — the G6c idiom (`CURRENT-STATUS.md:3222`):
  assert `executed ≤ charged` and record both.
- **Do not charge the digests.** After J1+J2 they are O(1) and O(delta); pricing them would be
  pricing an implementation detail, exactly the mistake G3 refused when it declined to charge the
  prefix.

**Sequencing is not negotiable:** charging first would enshrine the defect as a budget. FU-8b's
whole lesson (`support-resource-caps-2026-08-05.md:255–259`) is that the cap is not the remedy.

## D4 — J0, first and separate: make a killed run reportable

FU-32's own first action (`CURRENT-STATUS.md:5244`). Give `plinth_support::census` a
**periodic flush** (every *n* seconds or every *n* records, line-buffered to stderr) **and** a
`SIGTERM`/`SIGINT` handler that calls the same flush path; change the watchdog to send `SIGTERM`,
wait, then `SIGKILL`. Not hashed, not consumed by production code, feature- and runtime-gated —
the `coalesced_seat_count` / `MergeTournamentCensus` precedent (`CURRENT-STATUS.md:4699`).
This design's own §M is the existence proof that it works.

# MEMORY DISCIPLINE — RESTATED, AND BINDING ON J6

Every treant run under this design obeys the standing memory protocol without deviation:

- **release** build, `--features census`, `PLINTH_CENSUS=1`, `PLINTH_CENSUS_ONLY="Weeping Treant v1.2"`, `PLINTH_THREADS=4`;
- an **RSS watchdog running UNSANDBOXED**, polling every 2 s, hard-armed at **90 GB** — with J0 it
  sends `SIGTERM` first so the census flushes, then `SIGKILL`;
- a **600 s timebox**;
- **two attempts and no more**, machine-quiet evidence recorded at each launch (`ps` showing no
  other `cargo`/`rustc`/`embed_incidence_census`; load averages; core count and RAM);
- **EXCEEDS-with-counters is a valid, recordable outcome** — and after J0 it is finally *achievable*;
- the **~100 GB working ceiling on a 128 GB machine is a finding, not a limit to raise**. Nothing
  in this design goes near it: measured peak is **3,704 MiB**, projected ≈4.3 GiB, **under 5 % of
  the arm**;
- **cargo is always run unsandboxed**;
- **run outputs never enter git.** The scratch clone at `/tmp/claude-501/plinth-fu32`, the
  per-terminal logs and the RSS traces are ephemeral by construction, exactly as G4's precedent
  requires (`CURRENT-STATUS.md`, the G4 scratch-transcript ruling). The record of this design is
  this document and the tables in §M.
